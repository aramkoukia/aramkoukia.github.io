### What does the next 2 to 3 years look like for generative AI?

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*9KdhdbXc3lYylGVT.jpg)*Image from <https://arstechnica.com/>*

Generative AI seems to be everywhere these days and the bets are, this is jut the beginning and it will be more present in our lives in several industries. Here are just a few to expect:

In the next 2 to 3 years, we can expect to see an increase in the adoption of generative AI across several industries including:

1. **Healthcare:** Generative AI can help in drug discovery, personalized treatment plans and analysis of medical images.
2. **Art & Design:** AI-powered tools will become more accessible to artists, designers and marketers for creating unique visual content.
3. **E-commerce:** AI models can generate product descriptions, customer reviews, and product recommendations.
4. **Finance:** AI-powered tools will be used for fraud detection, credit scoring, and financial forecasting.
5. **Manufacturing:** Generative AI can help in creating optimized production schedules, improving supply chain management, and prototyping new products.

However, it is important to note that AI adoption will also depend on the specific limitations and ethical considerations of the industry, as well as the progress of AI research and development.

#### How long until the government will try to regulate generative AI?

It’s difficult to predict exactly when governments will start to regulate generative AI, as it depends on a variety of factors such as the pace of AI development, public opinion, and government priorities. However, with the increasing use and potential impact of generative AI, it is likely that governments will start taking regulatory measures in the near future.

In recent years, governments around the world have started taking steps to regulate AI and its applications. For example, the European Union has introduced the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) which affects AI applications, and some countries have started to consider regulations specifically targeting AI.

It’s important for governments to balance the benefits and potential risks of generative AI, while also considering the impact on businesses, citizens, and the overall economy. Overall, it can be expected that the regulation of generative AI will be a gradual process, shaped by a combination of technical advancements and public discourse.
